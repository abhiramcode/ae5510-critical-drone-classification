This project involves developing a critical node identification algorithm for identifying agents that play a critical role in maintaining the connectivity, coordination, and operational effectiveness of an aerial swarm.

A dataset containing agent-level observations collected from simulated aerial swarm scenarios under varying swarm sizes, obstacle configurations, and attrition conditions are provided. The dataset includes information related to the simulation scenario, environment, agent kinematics, swarm geometry, neighboring agents, graph-based connectivity, and agent criticality.

The project will begin by identifying the key factors associated with agent criticality. Relevant features describing agent motion, relative positions, swarm geometry, and graph connectivity will be analyzed and preprocessed to establish a suitable representation for critical node identification. The dataset will subsequently be divided into appropriate training, validation, and testing sets to enable systematic development and evaluation of the proposed algorithm.

Following data preprocessing, a suitable machine learning or graph-based algorithm should be selected and trained to identify critical nodes within the aerial swarm. Approaches based on graph neural networks, node classification, ensemble learning, or other suitable techniques may be investigated. The algorithm will be trained using the agent-level observations and corresponding criticality information, with particular emphasis on capturing both local interactions between neighboring agents and the overall connectivity structure of the swarm.

The developed critical node identification algorithm will then be evaluated using the validation and testing sets. Appropriate classification and ranking metrics, such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and area under the precision-recall curve, will be considered to quantify the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Where appropriate, additional metrics for ranking the most critical agents may also be investigated. The evaluation will consider different swarm sizes, obstacle configurations, and levels of agent attrition to assess the generalization and robustness of the algorithm under varying operational conditions.

The analysis will further investigate how changes in swarm structure and agent attrition influence the identification of critical nodes. The results will provide a systematic evaluation of critical node identification in aerial swarms and establish the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed algorithm under diverse simulated operating conditions.
